Philip Nkwam

Computational Imaging Researcher

I work on the part of brain imaging that decides whether a number can be trusted. That means reconstructing PET straight from raw detector events, measuring how much tracer dose and scan time a reliable amyloid reading really needs, and building tools like WebMRIQC so researchers without a supercomputer can run the same science. Most of it comes back to one question: what still works when scanners, tracer and compute are scarce?

about

I'm a computational imaging researcher working on reconstruction, uncertainty quantification and deep learning for quantitative PET and MRI. This summer I was a visiting researcher at the UCL Hawkes Institute and the Queen Square Institute of Neurology, hosted by the MANIFOLD Lab and supervised by Prof David Cash, Prof James Cole and Dr Udunna Anazodo, building a low-dose amyloid PET pipeline from raw list-mode data. I'm also a research assistant at MAILAB and Deep Learning Research Coordinator at CAMERA MRI Africa's SPARK Academy.

I trained as a diagnostic radiographer at the College of Medicine, University of Lagos, graduating as Overall Best Graduating Student in the research category. Along the way I led the external validation of MIT's MIRAI breast-cancer risk model on African mammograms, led a 12-member team at BraTS-Africa, and built WebMRIQC, my undergraduate thesis, now accepted at MICCAI MIRASOL 2026. The University of Lagos told that story in “From Mammograms to Memory Maps”.

I care about the people who will run this science next. After a year as an ISTAART Ambassador in 2025, I became Global Lead Ambassador for the Alzheimer's Association's ISTAART programme in 2026, the first undergraduate and first African in the role, and I'm an early-career representative on its Neuroimaging PIA executive committee. I founded Brain-AID for students at my institution who had no route into neuroimaging research, and taught machine learning to more than 150 fellows at The Bulb Africa.

    Learning from the giants

    Much of what I took away happened away from the keyboard: reading low- and high-field MRI with Prof Frederik Barkhof at the Dementia Research Centre; meeting Prof Henrik Zetterberg at AAIC 2026; a day in Prof Rimona Weil's clinic at UCLH, following the stage-by-stage diagnosis of Lewy body and Parkinson's disease dementia; normative modelling with Dr Neil Oxtoby, and ultra-fast MR with Prof Geoff Parker's group.

    // spotlight

    Prof Frederik Barkhof and Prof Henrik Zetterberg are two of the most renowned neuroscientists in the world. Barkhof, Chair of Neuroradiology at UCL, gave his name to the MRI criteria that changed how multiple sclerosis is diagnosed and has been listed among the world's most influential scientists. Zetterberg, Professor of Neurochemistry at UCL and the University of Gothenburg, leads the UK DRI Fluid Biomarker Laboratory and is one of Clarivate's Highly Cited Researchers. Learning from both in the same summer is not something I take for granted.

projects

low-dose amyloid PET2026

How little dose does amyloid PET need?

Philip Nkwam · with David Cash, James Cole and Udunna Anazodo, UCL

An end-to-end pipeline that reconstructs [¹⁸F]florbetapir PET/MR from raw list-mode events with NiftyPET, then thins the data in time and resamples it with a Poisson bootstrap to find where regional amyloid measurements stop being trustworthy. More than 12,000 GPU reconstructions, a cross-tracer deep-learning denoiser judged against an empirically measured noise floor, and validation against an independent reconstruction of the same Insight 46 cohort.

MANIFOLD Lab ↗ UCL Hawkes Institute ↗ NiftyPET ↗
MRI quality control2025 – Present

WebMRIQC ↗

Philip Nkwam, Ifeoluwa Oladeji, Sekinat Zurakat-Aderibigbe … Udunna Anazodo

MRIQC, the field standard for automated MRI quality assessment, assumes an HPC cluster. WebMRIQC runs it from the browser instead, with DICOM-to-BIDS conversion built in and SNR, CNR, FD and DVARS extracted in 5–10 minutes per T1 over an ordinary connection. Used in the SWiM Program 2026; my undergraduate thesis, accepted at MICCAI MIRASOL 2026.

Tool ↗ Paper ↗ MAILAB ↗
external validation2022 – 2025

Does MIRAI hold in Africa?

Philip Nkwam, Ifeoluwa Amure, Maruf Adewole … Kingsley Ndoh · with MIT CSAIL

External validation of MIRAI, a deep-learning breast-cancer risk model developed at MIT CSAIL and the Jameel Clinic, on African mammography it never saw in training. Curated 3,325 patients across six institutions in Nigeria, Kenya and Uganda down to 89 high-quality paired scans, and traced a loss of performance to missing view-position metadata. Run with the St Cyril Cancer Treatment Foundation and funded by a ₦6M ACTS Foundation grant; the MICCAI Open Data Large Grant (2026) will publish the data openly.
